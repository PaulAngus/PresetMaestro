# FM9 amp identity review

Reviewed 331 supplied entries: 307 real-amp/basis entries in 139 families, and 24 virtual-original entries in 23 groups. 329 original entries have device-confirmed numeric joins; two combined/different names have no accepted join. The follow-up additionally maps the exact selectable USA MK IV Lead and Fox ODS Mid names. All 336 names returned by the FM9 12.00 device table map to 162 runtime family/original groups; arbitrary unknown scan IDs still appear under Unmapped.

Review date: 2026-10-02. The supplied CSV is unchanged. [Machine-readable row-by-row evidence](fm9-12-amp-identities.json).

Porta-Bass attribution updated on 2026-10-03 using the archived Ares 12.05 model table; catalogue version 2026-10-03.1.

Catalogue 2026-10-03.2 groups PVH 6160 Block Crunch under Peavey 5150 Block Letter, giving Fractal's official product-family attribution precedence over a conflicting third-party guide.

## What was checked

Every supplied name was reconciled with its Ampdex entry and checked against the Wiki manufacturer/model table and relevant model sections. Each row cites both. The official Fractal manual and Fullerton product table provide additional primary support where applicable. The Wiki was accessible through indexed excerpts, not a successful live full-page fetch.

**Two citations do not mean two independent confirmations.** Ampdex transcribes Welch, whose guide draws on Yek, the Wiki and Fractal forum. The JSON records this shared lineage. “Corroborated” means the manufacturer/product identification is supported across the checked sources; it does not certify every supplied year, circuit or switch setting.

The historical manual also contains mistakes and outdated descriptions (including channel swaps and Recto2 generation labels). Conflicts are retained below rather than settled by counting matching copies. Physical modified amps, schematic interpretations and virtual originals have separate relationship types.

Explicit official Fractal product attributions take precedence over conflicting third-party guides. Each source's scope is retained: the historic Block manual row describes the lead channel; the current Wiki supplies the later Crunch channel detail. Neither attribution independently verifies a current DSP ID.

## Follow-up research and remaining boundaries

Catalogue 2026-10-03.3 applies the user's supplied ChatGPT research table. [Follow-up findings and source limitations](amp-attribution-followup.md) distinguish directly checked Fractal statements, community descriptions, reproduced release notes and inference. The original CSV and its record names remain unchanged. All five previously unjoined selectable candidate names now have manufacturer/product mappings; at that stage, attribution alone did not verify their DSP IDs or firmware availability. The subsequent 2026-10-03.4 direct device read confirms all 336 ID/name pairs for FM9 12.00 independently of attribution research.

- Porta-Bass: B-15 Portaflex is documented; B-15R reissue is a control-layout inference, not an explicit Fractal designation. Exact reference year is undocumented.
- Princetone Reverb: 1966 blackface Princeton Reverb; AA1164-family circuit is inferred, with the conflicting Wiki AA964 label retained.
- Fox ODS Mid: Fuchs ODS-50 identity accepted; exact Mid-switch meaning is firmware-specific. It is not joined to the CSV Fox ODS Deep row.
- ODS-100 Clean: Dumble ODS/Clean established; current Ford and historical HRM serial 0213 descriptions remain distinct. Exact FM9 12 reference is not established.
- USA MK IV Lead: Mark IV Lead accepted; Rev B remains probable, not proven. The CSV LEAD/RHYTHM heading is retained separately.
- Brit 800 2203: 100W High/Low input accepted; exact JMP/JCM800 production badge/year remains open.
- AC30 Bright/Brilliant: non-Top-Boost Bright and DC-30 tone-stack Brilliant descriptions accepted from supplied research with source scope retained; exact years are undocumented.
- Brit Silver: 2555 100W accepted from supplied research; cited administrator page was not independently confirmed in this follow-up.
- Friedman BE/HBE 2010: early Marsha platform accepted; IDs 287/288 are now device-confirmed. They remain separate from later BE-100 models.
- Vibrato Lux: 1963 is preferred from reproduced original release text; that repost is not independently authenticated. Vibrolux Reverb is not substituted.

## Application and firmware scope

The browser now uses the complete 336-name table returned directly by the FM9 running firmware 12.00. Both exact names and explicitly reviewed aliases join to those device-confirmed IDs. Numeric IDs were never derived from CSV order or Ampdex numbers. The 331-row input alone did not prove firmware availability or completeness. [Raw device evidence](fm9-12-device-roster.json) now supplies independent numeric confirmation, including 233, 287 and 288 and the firmware-12 additions. FM3, Axe-Fx III and other FM9 firmware versions require separate evidence.

Family cards preserve separate reissues, Rectifier generations, 5150 III wattages/Stealth, Legacy generations, VH4 revisions, Dumble bases, modified JCM800s and different Plexi reference amps. Channel/switch variants of the same reference amp share a card.
